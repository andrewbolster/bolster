# Insights Digest - 2026-10-17

Scope: NISRA monthly vital-event releases published 12 Oct 2026 (births, marriages, civil partnerships, stillbirths, monthly deaths) and the weekly deaths releases of 9 Oct 2026 (week ending 9 Oct). All numbers below were produced by running the library against live data on 2026-10-17. Association only, no causal claims.

## 1. Registered births keep falling, but the decline has nearly stopped

- Births registered in the 12 months to Sep 2026: **18,934**. Same 12-month window one, two and three years earlier: 19,091 (2025), 19,574 (2024), 20,202 (2023).
- Year-on-year change: 2024 -3.1%, 2025 -2.5%, 2026 **-0.8%**. Versus the 12 months to Sep 2014 (24,205): **-21.8%**.
- Cross-check on a different basis: Jan-Jul 2026 births by month of occurrence 10,830 vs 10,961 in 2025 (-1.2%); by registration 11,078 vs 11,215 (-1.2%). Both bases agree on direction and rough size (the 12-month figure above has one more quarter of data, hence -0.8%).
- Sex ratio (males per female, registrations) 2026 YTD 1.046 vs 1.067 in 2025; year-to-year swings of this size are routine at these counts and are not a finding.

```python
from bolster.data_sources.nisra import births
r = births.get_latest_births()["registration"]
r = r[r.sex == "Persons"].set_index("month").births
print(r.rolling(12).sum().loc[[f"{y}-09-01" for y in (2014, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026)]])
```

Vintage: monthly file to Sep 2026 (registration) and Jul 2026 (occurrence). Geography: Northern Ireland, mother's residence.
Caveat: registration month is not birth month (most are registered within 42 days); a flattening over one 12-month window is not a turning point.
Confidence: **high** on level and long-run fall, **medium** on "flattening".

## 2. Marriage registrations Jan-Sep 2026 are up 6.7% on 2025, the highest since 2022

- Jan-Sep marriages registered: 2026 **6,036**, 2025 5,655, 2024 5,760, 2023 5,920, 2022 6,960 (post-Covid catch-up), 2019 5,786, 2018 6,288.
- Month by month vs 2025: Jan -4.9%, Feb -0.8%, **Mar +34.7% (427 vs 317)**, Apr +2.3%, May -4.5%, Jun +10.4%, Jul +11.7%, Aug +3.3%, Sep +10.6%.
- The March jump alone is about 110 of the ~380 extra registrations; the rest is spread across summer months.

```python
from bolster.data_sources.nisra import marriages
m = marriages.get_latest_marriages().dropna().set_index("date").marriages
print(m[m.index.month <= 9].groupby(m[m.index.month <= 9].index.year).sum().tail(8))
```

Vintage: monthly file, provisional for 2026. Geography: Northern Ireland.
Caveat: the series counts month of **registration**, not ceremony, and 2026 is provisional. Month-level swings (notably March) can reflect calendar effects such as the timing of Easter or registration backlog; the data here cannot confirm which, so treat that as a hypothesis only.
Confidence: **medium**.

## 3. Natural-change cushion narrowing: births still exceed deaths by ~9%, but the surplus is smaller than in 2024

- Births registered Jan-Sep vs deaths registered in 39 weekly releases covering the same span (weeks ending ~9 Jan to ~2 Oct):
  - 2024: 14,811 births vs 13,376 deaths, surplus 1,435 (ratio 1.107)
  - 2025: 14,486 vs 13,253, surplus 1,233 (1.093)
  - 2026: 14,358 vs 13,138, surplus **1,220** (1.093)
- The surplus is 15% smaller than in 2024. Between 2025 and 2026 it is essentially unchanged, because births and deaths both fell by roughly 1%.

```python
from bolster.data_sources.nisra import births, deaths
# births: registration, Persons, Jan-Sep; deaths: first 39 week_ending dates on/after 7 Jan
t = deaths.get_latest_deaths()["totals"]
```

Mismatches (important): this is a registration-based comparison, not a demographic natural-change estimate. (a) Weeks end on Fridays so the death window is offset from Jan-Sep by up to six days; (b) births are by mother's residence, weekly deaths are registrations in NI and are not defined by residence in this module; (c) weekly data only begins in 2024 so no longer baseline is possible; (d) migration is not included. Do not read this as population change.
Confidence: **low-medium**.

## 4. Deaths are running below NISRA's five-year expectation for the year to date

- Weeks 1-41, observed deaths: 2026 **13,784** vs 14,333 expected (-3.8%); 2025 14,013 vs 14,533 (-3.6%); 2024 14,049 vs 14,350 (-2.1%).
- 16 of the 41 weeks in 2026 were above expectation (week 40, ending 2 Oct, was +31; the latest week, ending 9 Oct, was -9).
- Flu/pneumonia-coded deaths are 16.9% of weeks 1-41 deaths in 2026 (2,332 of 13,784) vs 17.9% in 2025 (2,516 of 14,013) and 18.3% in 2024; COVID-coded deaths 40 YTD vs 104 (2025) and 319 (2024).

```python
from bolster.data_sources.nisra import deaths
t = deaths.get_latest_deaths()["totals"]
t["y"] = t.week_ending.astype("datetime64[ns]").dt.year
print(t[t.week_number <= 41].groupby("y")[["observed_deaths", "expected_deaths_5yr", "flu_pneumonia_deaths", "covid_deaths"]].sum())
```

Vintage: weekly, to week ending 9 Oct 2026. Geography: Northern Ireland.
Caveat: the module does not document how the five-year baseline is built, so a persistent negative gap may partly reflect a baseline that includes elevated years; also the series starts in 2024 so there is no local history to test that. Cause-of-death coding fields are overlapping categories, not a partition.
Confidence: **medium** for the observed counts, **low** for the interpretation of the gap.

## Joins considered and rejected

- **Births vs stillbirths**: the stillbirths module returns annual figures by HSCT only up to 2024 (60 in NI), so it does not reflect the October monthly release, differs in frequency, and uses a different event basis from monthly registered births. No rate was computed.
- **Marriages vs births (lag)**: tempting as "marriages lead births", but marriages are by registration month in NI while births are by mother's residence; with no marital-status or parity breakdown in either module the link is not testable.
- **Civil partnerships vs marriages share**: Jan-Sep civil partnerships were 46 in 2026 and 53 in 2025 vs 26-31 in 2021-24 and 62-87 in 2014-2019. The counts are too small, and the legal framework governing both has changed over the period, so ratios are noise-prone and not reported as a finding.
- **Weekly deaths by LGD vs births by district**: the births module has no sub-NI geography.
